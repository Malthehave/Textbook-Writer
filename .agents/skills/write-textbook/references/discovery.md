# Discover the book the learner needs

A learner may ask vaguely to understand a subject or a real system. Treat that as enough
to start a conversation, not as a request for generic introductory coverage. High-quality
research, explanations, illustrations, practice and review are the workflow default;
the learner does not need to request each of them.

## Read context first

Read output/learner/persona.md when present. Combine it with this conversation and supplied
references. A new conversation has no automatic access to previous conversations; rely on
saved context, not imagined memory. Current learner statements override inferred profile
claims. Distinguish known knowledge, stated preferences and uncertain assumptions.

## Ask only questions that change the book

Begin by reflecting the intended learning outcome in plain language. Ask a small batch of
focused questions, usually two or three, about the gaps that matter: what they hope to
understand or do, their starting knowledge in the relevant domain, or the practical system
they want to follow. Adapt follow-ups to their answers; do not administer a fixed survey.
Do not ask them to choose agents, schemas, research methods or quality gates.

Propose a depth and approximate length that can deliver the outcome. Explain any tradeoff
between a conceptual explanation and hands-on engineering depth. Do not inherit a five-page
pilot scope or another book's target length. The reader may approve a recommendation or
request a shorter scope. Confirm the learning promise before subject research begins.
When the user has already supplied and confirmed enough context, proceed without another
approval ritual. Refine the exact chapter structure after researching the subject.

## Turn preferences into authoring decisions

Record in brief.json a personalization object containing known starting knowledge, relevant
gaps, teaching preferences, concrete adaptations, assumptions and evidence from the learner.
For example, a preference for intuition can mean explaining a mechanism with a small worked
example before notation; a preference for visual explanation can mean planning figures at
conceptual transitions. These are adaptable decisions, not a mandatory formula per section.
Pass the brief to authors, diagram designers and reviewers. Review the finished manuscript
against it, rather than merely mentioning the learner's name or interests.

Save newly confirmed durable learning preferences to the local persona, preserving existing
substance and distinguishing direct feedback from inference. Topic goals, model choices,
company examples and page targets belong in the book brief, not the durable persona. Do
not encode a single misunderstanding as a permanent learner trait or repeated example.

## Quality contract

Simple input should yield a thoughtful interview and a substantial book when the goal
requires one. Preserve researched coverage, causal explanations, sufficient scaffolding,
coherent examples, meaningful illustrations and independently solved practice. Do not
equate beginner-friendly with shallow. After the first book, invite feedback and translate
it into general improvements without overfitting to a particular passage.
